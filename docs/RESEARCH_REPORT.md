# SWARMOS Research Report: Resilient Task Allocation

## 1. Scientific Thesis
SWARMOS improves decentralized task-allocation resilience under communication degradation and agent attrition through lightweight recovery, while a physically grounded anomaly detector provides limited protection against physically inconsistent adversarial behavior.

## 2. Methodology (P0)
We benchmark SWARMOS against the B0-B5 baseline ladder:
- **B2 (Standard)**: Choi 2009 CBBA.
- **B3 (Recovery)**: CBBA with heartbeat-driven orphaned task recovery.
- **B4 (Filter)**: CBBA with kinematic anomaly detection.
- **B5 (SWARMOS)**: The full integrated architecture.

### Adversarial Model
We inject a fraction $f \in \{0 \dots 0.5\}$ of attackers, where $n = \max(1, \text{round}(f \cdot N))$. 
- **Class D**: Intermittent poisoning with 10s temporal cycles (Honest/Poison).

## 3. Results (P1)
Results are audited using pure-python implementations of the **Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test** with **Holm-Bonferroni correction**.

### Resilience Boundary (Failure Envelope)
We define the resilience boundary as the region in the $(p, f)$ space where $P(\text{TCR} \ge 0.9) > 0.5$.

### Optimality Guarantee
We maintain the theoretical $R_{\text{opt}} \ge 0.5$ lower bound across all non-catastrophic configurations.

## 4. Conclusion
While SWARMOS does not "defeat" Byzantine nodes in the cryptographic sense, it provides a sufficient physical boundary for mission continuity in contested, kinematic-constrained environments.
